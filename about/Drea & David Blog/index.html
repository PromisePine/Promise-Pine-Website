The Dunafon Castle Wedding | Drea & David | September 27, 2025

September 27, 2025 | Dunafon Castle | Jefferson County, Colorado Wedding Film

Tucked away in Jefferson County near Red Rocks Amphitheatre, The Dunafon Castle is easily one of the most aesthetically stunning wedding venues in Colorado. With cascading red vines climbing the castle walls, peaceful water features weaving through the grounds, and a willow tree filtering golden fall light across the ceremony space, it feels like something out of a European storybook — right here in the Colorado mountains.

Drea and David's September 27th wedding embraced that magic completely.

The day was wrapped in golden autumn light and a crisp Colorado breeze. Sunlight filtered through the trees, casting soft, speckled shadows across the stone courtyard. It was elegant. Romantic. And yet somehow playful at the same time — exactly like the two of them.

A Grand Yet Intimate Ceremony

Though their ceremony felt grand and modern in scale, it was deeply personal and non-traditional — perfectly suited to who they are as a couple.

Before walking down the aisle, Drea and David shared private vows. No one else heard what was said, but you didn't need to. Their expressions told the whole story. There was an intimacy in that moment that felt sacred — the kind of connection that reminds you why wedding films matter.

It's easy for weddings at a venue this breathtaking to lean into pure aesthetic. But this day was never about being pristine or performative. It was about partnership.

And you can tell these two were made for each other.

Their dynamic is playful and teasing — multiple times throughout the day they jokingly flashed each other a middle finger, followed by laughter that felt completely unfiltered and genuine. There's something powerful about a couple who can be elegant and irreverent at the same time.

A Perfect Fall Palette

The styling could not have been more fitting for the season.

The bridesmaids wore deep red gowns that echoed the vines climbing the castle walls. The groomsmen stood in rich dark green suits — grounding the color palette in earthy autumn tones. Against the stone architecture and flowing water features, the entire wedding felt cohesive and intentional.

It was refined without feeling rigid.

A Rotary Phone & The Power of Audio

One of the most unique elements of the day was waiting at the guest entrance.

An old rotary phone sat on a table, inviting family and friends to leave voice messages for the couple. Throughout the evening, guests picked up the receiver and recorded heartfelt (and sometimes hilarious) messages.

Drea and David graciously shared those recordings with me afterward, allowing me to weave them throughout their wedding film.

The result became a living motif — voices layered over transitions, laughter bridging scenes, emotion carrying from one moment to the next.

When Drea later messaged me, she said:

"We saw it and it was SPECTACULAR. I adore the way you interwove all the little audio clips, all the transitions, just loved it."

And:

"We were all blown away. It felt so true to the evening and personal. Highlighted the fun of the evening, versus being so focused on being pristine and commercial. Truly, thank you immensely for this."

That's always the goal.

Not commercial.
Not staged perfection.
But something that feels true.

Honored to Work Alongside Matt Wilson

I was brought on as the videographer just one week before the wedding — something that could have easily felt rushed or chaotic. Instead, it became one of those moments that reminds me why I love what I do.

I had the privilege of working alongside photographer Matt Wilson, whose mastery behind the lens is evident in every frame. His ability to coordinate portraits with clarity and confidence allowed the day to flow seamlessly.

His images from this wedding are impeccable, and I deeply respect the craft he's honed. Working with professionals who elevate the experience for the couple makes all the difference.

Between the flowing water, stone textures, towering trees, and golden Colorado light, The Dunafon Castle lends itself to a cinematic wedding film in a way few venues can.

A Wedding That Almost Wasn't Filmed

There's one more part of this story that makes it even more meaningful.

I was hired just one week before the wedding — by Drea's mother.

Originally, Drea and David hadn't planned on having a videographer at all. Their hesitation was understandable. Some wedding videographers arrive with large rigs, heavy equipment, and a noticeable production presence that can unintentionally shift focus away from the couple and the reason everyone gathered in the first place.

A wedding should never feel like a film set.

It should feel like a wedding.

When Drea's mother reached out, she shared that they wanted the day to remain centered on the celebration — not on cameras or production.

That philosophy is exactly why Promise Pine exists.

Promise Pine wedding films are filmed quietly, allowing the day to unfold naturally without disruption. Every film is crafted with intention, centered on the couple and the moments that matter most to them. The result is an honest, cinematic story that reflects who you are and how it truly felt to be there.

By the end of the night, the focus was never on the camera.

It was on Drea and David — laughing, exchanging glances, playfully teasing each other, and celebrating the start of their marriage.

And now, they have a film that preserves all of it.
