What Makes a Wedding Film Cinematic? Aspect Ratios & Emotional Storytelling in Colorado

Cinematic Framing & Emotional Storytelling

1. Cinematic Framing (Yes, Aspect Ratio Matters)

Aspect ratio — the relationship between width and height in a frame — subtly changes how a moment feels.

Most online video uses 16:9. It's standard. Functional. Familiar.

But cinema has always experimented beyond that.

Common Cinema Aspect Ratios

IMAX, often shot on massive 70mm film, uses a taller frame that fills more of your vision vertically. Interestingly, that reflects a broader cultural shift. For decades, cinema pushed wider and wider. Now we're seeing a renewed appreciation for height — for immersion that surrounds rather than stretches.

That mirrors how we consume media today:

  • Phones are vertical.
  • Stories are vertical.
  • Moments are framed upright.

The frame is getting taller again.

And in weddings, taller frames often feel more intimate.

2. Why I Love 4:3 for Emotional Moments

4:3 has a way of pulling you inward.

There's less empty space. Faces dominate the frame. Background distractions fall away. It feels closer — almost like flipping through a memory rather than watching a production.

In wedding films, that can transform the experience:

  • Vows feel personal instead of performative
  • First looks feel intimate instead of staged
  • Reactions feel human, not cinematic spectacle

Wider isn't always more emotional.

Sometimes taller feels more honest.

3. Subtle Width: 1.85:1 Over 16:9

While 16:9 is today's default, it can feel… neutral.

Television-like.

That's why even small shifts matter.

1.85:1 is only slightly wider, but it introduces separation from the everyday. It feels more intentional. More immersive.

In one of my past projects, I opted for 1.85:1 instead of 16:9 specifically to create that subtle cinematic distinction. The difference is small on paper — but emotionally, it changes how the frame breathes.

Cinematic filmmaking often lives in those subtle choices.

4. Cinematic Movement

Aspect ratio alone doesn't create cinema.

Movement does.

  • Slow, controlled camera motion
  • Natural handheld energy when appropriate
  • Letting moments breathe instead of rushing cuts

In Colorado especially — with mountain backdrops, open skies, and dramatic landscapes — movement interacts with space. A wider frame emphasizes scale. A taller frame emphasizes presence.

Both can be powerful. The key is choosing intentionally.

5. Cinematic Editing

Cinematic doesn't mean long.

It means purposeful.

  • Letting silence exist
  • Building pacing around emotion
  • Using audio (vows, toasts, ambient sound) as narrative anchors
  • Cutting based on feeling rather than just chronology

A cinematic wedding film feels like a story unfolding — not an event being summarized.

6. Cinematic Isn't a Trend

The word "cinematic" gets overused.

But at its core, it simply means this:

The film was crafted — not just recorded.

It means someone thought about:

  • Framing
  • Negative space
  • Emotional proximity
  • How the image shape influences feeling

Whether that's through 4:3 for intimacy, 1.85:1 for immersion, or thoughtful composition within any ratio — the goal is always the same:

To make you feel like you're back inside the moment.

Why This Matters for Colorado Weddings

Colorado weddings are unique.

They're often outdoors. Expansive. Textural. Emotional against dramatic landscapes.

A cinematic wedding film here isn't just about showing the mountains.

It's about balancing scale with intimacy.

Your film should feel both epic and personal.

That balance often begins with something as simple — and as powerful — as the shape of the frame.
